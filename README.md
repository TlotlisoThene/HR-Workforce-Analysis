# 1. Project Overview

This project presents an interactive **HR Analytics Dashboard** developed in **Power BI** to help Human Resources stakeholders monitor employee attrition, workforce demographics, salary distribution, and departmental performance.

The dashboard transforms raw HR data into meaningful insights, enabling organizations to identify workforce trends, understand the key drivers of employee turnover, and support data-driven workforce planning and retention strategies.

# 2. Business Background

Employee attrition is one of the biggest challenges faced by organizations. High turnover increases recruitment costs, reduces productivity, and affects overall business performance.

HR departments require timely insights into employee demographics, job roles, education levels, salaries, and years of service to better understand why employees leave and to develop effective retention strategies.

An interactive HR dashboard allows decision-makers to monitor workforce metrics, identify high-risk employee groups, and make informed human resource decisions.

# 3. Business Problem

The Human Resources department needed a centralized reporting solution to better understand employee attrition across the organization.

Management wanted to answer key business questions such as:

- Which employee groups experience the highest attrition?
- Which job roles have the highest employee turnover?
- Does salary influence employee attrition?
- Which age groups are more likely to leave?
- How does education level affect employee retention?
- Which departments require immediate retention strategies?

# 4. Business Objectives

The primary objectives of this project are to:

- Analyze employee attrition across different demographic groups.
- Identify job roles with the highest employee turnover.
- Examine the relationship between salary levels and employee attrition.
- Evaluate how age, education, and years at the company influence employee retention.
- Monitor key HR metrics such as employee count, average salary, average age, and attrition rate.
- Develop an interactive dashboard that enables HR managers to monitor workforce trends and support strategic decision-making.

# 6. Methodology

## 1. Data Preparation

The HR dataset was imported into Power BI and prepared for analysis.

### Data Cleaning

- Imported the employee dataset.
- Validated data quality and checked for missing values.
- Standardized column formats and data types.
- Removed duplicate records where necessary.
- Prepared the dataset for reporting and visualization.

### Data Modeling

The dataset was modeled to support efficient dashboard performance.

Key calculations included:

- Employee Count
- Attrition Count
- Attrition Rate
- Average Age
- Average Salary
- Average Years at Company

Additional calculated measures were created using DAX to analyze employee attrition across multiple dimensions.

## 2. Dashboard Development

An interactive Power BI dashboard was designed to provide HR stakeholders with an overview of workforce performance and employee attrition.

The dashboard enables users to:

- Monitor employee attrition.
- Analyze workforce demographics.
- Compare attrition across job roles.
- Evaluate salary distribution.
- Analyze employee tenure.
- Filter insights by department.

# 7. Key Insights

The dashboard analysis revealed several important workforce insights:

- The organization has **1,470 employees**, with an overall **attrition rate of 16.1%** and **237 employees** leaving the company.
- Employees aged **26–35 years** experienced the highest attrition, accounting for **116 employees**, indicating that early-career professionals are the most likely to leave.
- Employees with a **Life Sciences** educational background recorded the highest attrition (**38%**), followed by **Medical (27%)** and **Marketing (15%)**.
- **Laboratory Technicians** experienced the highest employee turnover (**62 employees**), followed by **Sales Executives (57)** and **Research Scientists (47)**.
- Employees earning **up to 5K** represented the highest attrition group (**163 employees**), suggesting that lower salary levels may contribute to higher employee turnover.
- Male employees accounted for **140 attrition cases**, compared to **79 female employees**, indicating higher employee turnover among males.
- Employee attrition was highest during the early years of employment, with a noticeable decline as employee tenure increased.


# 8. Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the following recommendations were made:

### Improve Early Employee Retention

Develop structured onboarding, mentoring, and career development programs to reduce attrition among employees in their first few years.

### Review Compensation Strategy

Evaluate salary structures for lower salary bands to improve employee satisfaction and reduce turnover.

### Focus on High-Attrition Job Roles

Implement targeted retention initiatives for Laboratory Technicians, Sales Executives, and Research Scientists.

### Enhance Career Development

Provide learning opportunities, promotion pathways, and professional development programs to encourage long-term employee retention.

### Monitor High-Risk Employee Groups

Regularly monitor employees within the 26–35 age group and lower salary brackets to proactively identify retention risks.

### Strengthen Workforce Planning

Use dashboard insights to support recruitment planning, succession planning, and departmental workforce management.

# 9. Challenges & Limitations

### Challenges

- Preparing employee data for accurate reporting.
- Creating DAX measures for HR KPIs and attrition calculations.
- Designing a dashboard that balances detailed workforce analysis with usability.
- Ensuring visualizations clearly communicate employee attrition trends.

### Limitations

- The analysis is based on historical HR data and may not fully predict future employee turnover.
- External factors such as employee satisfaction, organizational culture, and economic conditions were not included in the dataset.
- Additional employee engagement metrics could provide deeper insights into the causes of attrition.
